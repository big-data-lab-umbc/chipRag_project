# Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) offer promising capabilities for technical support but often lack institutional knowledge required for reliable high-performance computing (HPC) user assistance. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) incorporates external knowledge into LLM responses, but its effectiveness may depend on the knowledge source. This study presents chipRAG, an institutional HPC support benchmarking framework investigating how knowledge sources affect expert-perceived response quality. We evaluated two LLM models, namely Qwen2.5-3B and Mistral-7B, under four configurations: No RAG, historical support-ticket RAG, procedural Wiki RAG, and combined ticket+Wiki RAG. Eight LLM configurations and a human reference were assessed using 25 HPC support questions and seven criteria: relevance, HPC specificity, actionability, correctness, site-policy compliance, hallucination avoidance, and noise-copying avoidance. Five HPC experts completed 40 survey submissions, yielding 6,982 valid criterion-level ratings on a five-point Likert scale. Friedman tests and planned Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon signed-rank comparisons examined differences among configurations and between LLM-generated and human reference responses. Results indicate that Wiki-based RAG generally received more favorable ratings on several technical criteria, particularly for Mistral-7B, whereas historical support-ticket RAG showed limited and inconsistent benefits. Combining support tickets and procedural Wiki documentation did not consistently outperform individual knowledge sources. Human reference responses received significantly higher ratings than all eight LLM configurations on five of seven evaluation criteria under criterion-level Holm adjustment. Differences in relevance were less consistent, while no significant differences were detected for actionability. These findings highlight the importance of knowledge-source selection and domain-specific expert evaluation when developing RAG-based HPC support systems.
